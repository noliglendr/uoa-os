# The 4 Immigration Forms: Simple Guide

Background reading for Gate 1. It helps you understand Udoka's answers.
Fees and filing addresses change often, so always check uscis.gov. This is not legal advice.

---

## The big picture: a family green card

Most family cases use all 4 forms together:

```
I-130 (prove the family relationship)
   ↓
I-485 (apply for the green card)  +  I-765 (work permit)  +  I-131 (travel permit)
   ↓
Biometrics (fingerprints/photo) → Interview → Green card
```

When the relative is already in the U.S. and a visa is available, the firm often files all 4 **in one package** (a "bundle"). This is the "bundles" in Udoka's memo. I-765 and I-131 can be filed with I-485 at no extra fee.

---

## I-130: Petition for Alien Relative
- **Who files:** a U.S. citizen or green card holder (the "petitioner").
- **For whom:** a family member (the "beneficiary"), such as a spouse, child, parent or sibling.
- **Purpose:** proves the family relationship. It is **step 1 only** and does not give a green card.
- **Typical documents:** proof of the petitioner's citizenship or status, proof of the relationship (marriage or birth certificate), and, for spouses, proof the marriage is real (photos, joint bills).
- **After filing:** receipt notice, then approval. The relative may need to wait for a visa number (Visa Bulletin).

## I-485: Application to Adjust Status (green card)
- **Who files:** the person who wants the green card, while **inside the U.S.**
- **Purpose:** become a permanent resident.
- **Typical documents:** passport, birth certificate, entry record, a medical exam (Form I-693), a sponsor's financial support form (Form I-864), and photos.
- **After filing:** receipt notice (I-797), biometrics appointment, possibly an interview, then a decision.

## I-765: Application for Employment Authorization (work permit)
- **Who files:** a person waiting on their green card (or other eligible categories).
- **Purpose:** get an EAD card so they can work legally while waiting.
- **Typical documents:** ID, photos, a copy of the I-485 receipt (if filed separately), and the eligibility category code.
- **After filing:** receipt, possibly biometrics, then the card is mailed (trackable online).

## I-131: Application for Travel Document
- **Who files:** a person waiting on their green card who needs to travel abroad, and others (refugees, green card holders staying abroad for a long time).
- **Purpose:** "advance parole", which is permission to leave and come back. **Leaving without it can cancel the green card application.**
- **Typical documents:** ID, photos, and proof of status or a pending application.
- **After filing:** receipt, biometrics, then the travel document.

## Common steps in every case (these become the process map)

1. Client contacts the firm
2. Firm checks eligibility and conflicts
3. Engagement letter and fee
4. Collect documents (checklist per form)
5. Prepare forms
6. Attorney review and client signature
7. File with USCIS (online or by mail)
8. Receipt notice, which **starts deadline tracking**
9. Biometrics appointment
10. RFE (Request for Evidence) if USCIS needs more, **with a strict reply deadline**
11. Interview (for I-485)
12. Decision, then close the file

## Words you'll hear
| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Petitioner | The citizen or green card holder who sponsors |
| Beneficiary | The family member being sponsored |
| Receipt notice (I-797) | USCIS letter confirming they got the form |
| Biometrics | Fingerprints and photo appointment |
| RFE | Request for Evidence: USCIS asks for more documents by a deadline |
| EAD | Work permit card (from I-765) |
| Advance parole | Travel permission (from I-131) |
| Visa Bulletin | Monthly list showing when visas are available |
| Bundle | Filing I-485 + I-765 + I-131 (often with I-130) together |
